No. 01Computation · Aug 2026

Monte Carlo simulation of radioactive decay

I sampled every particle at random in each time step, then validated the run against the analytic law N(t) = N₀e−λt and analyzed the residuals and shot noise. A convergence study from N₀ = 100 to 100,000 atoms confirmed the 1/√N error scaling expected of Monte Carlo methods.

  • Monte Carlo methods
  • Error analysis

Mapping a magnetic field

I mapped dipole field lines and measured field strength against distance with an analog Hall effect sensor, an Arduino and MATLAB data acquisition. Clean data took a great deal of hardware troubleshooting. In the same lab I studied triboelectric charging, built an electroscope and wired series and parallel DC circuits.

Instrument: optics of an AR heads-up display

First-order model

My marketing plan for a proposed AR heads-up display raised a physics question: how big is the virtual image, is it sharp enough, how much does it spare a driver from refocusing, and how bright must it be in daylight? Move the controls. The defaults are illustrative assumptions, not measured values.

Virtual image widthw = 2d tan(FOV/2)
Angular resolution
Eye refocus demandPhone at 0.4 m: 2.50 D. Distant road: about 0 D.
Display luminance at the eyeLd = (C − 1) T Lbg
Source luminance neededLs = Ld / η

Method. Image width from distance and field of view. Resolution is pixels divided by field of view, against the eye's limit of about 60 pixels per degree. Refocus demand is 1/d in diopters. Contrast C = (Ld + T Lbg) / (T Lbg), solved for the luminance the display must add, then divided by optical efficiency.

Instrument: a Gaussian laser beam through a lens

Paraxial model

A laser beam is not a ray. It has a waist, a spread and a depth of focus, and a lens cannot focus it to a point. Set the laser, then place a thin lens and watch where the new waist lands and how small it gets. Defaults are illustrative, similar to a small red HeNe-class laser.

Focused waist radius
Depth of focus (2 zR)
Waist behind the lens

Method. The complex beam parameter is q = z + i zR, with zR = π w₀² / (M² λ). A thin lens changes it by 1/q′ = 1/q − 1/f, and free space adds the distance. The radius follows from w² = −M² λ / (π Im(1/q)). The waist after the lens sits at −Re(q′), and the depth of focus is 2 Im(q′).
